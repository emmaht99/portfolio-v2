# WCAG 2.2 Level AA Accessibility Audit

**Site:** Emma H. Tandle — UX & Product Designer Portfolio
**Audit date:** 2026-08-07
**Standard:** Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2, Conformance Level AA
**Auditor:** Claude (Anthropic), code-based review with live browser verification
**Scope note:** Conformance to Level AA requires meeting **all Level A criteria in addition to all Level AA criteria**, so this audit evaluates both levels (55 success criteria total). Level AAA criteria are out of scope and not evaluated.

No files were modified as part of this audit.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This portfolio is a small, well-crafted Next.js site (5 route templates, 4 case studies) built with a clear design system, semantic landmarks, consistent focus styling, and thoughtful `prefers-reduced-motion` handling — the kind of foundational care that is often missing entirely from portfolio sites. That groundwork means most of what's broken here is fixable quickly and without a redesign.

Of the 55 applicable WCAG 2.2 Level A/AA success criteria, **26 pass**, **13 fail** (fully or partially), and **16 are not applicable** (no forms, no time-based media with meaningful audio content, no authentication, etc.).

The failures cluster into a few root causes rather than being scattered noise:

1. **Every page shares one `<title>`.** The homepage, Work archive, About, Contact, and all four case studies all render `Emma H. Tandle — UX & Product Designer` in the browser tab. This is a Level A failure ([2.4.2](#2.4.2)) and the single highest-leverage fix in this report.
2. **No skip link**, so keyboard and screen reader users re-traverse the same navigation on every page load ([2.4.1](#2.4.1)).
3. **The sticky header can visually cover the element that currently has keyboard focus** when tabbing down a page — a new WCAG 2.2 criterion ([2.4.11](#2.4.11)), confirmed by direct measurement in-browser.
4. **Heading levels are generated by content heuristics** (`ProcessTimeline`, `InterviewPanel`, `GuidelineList` all hard-code `<h4>`) rather than by document structure, producing a heading outline that jumps from `<h2>` straight to `<h4>` and, on the Caritas Fællesskab case study, repeats the same label at two different heading levels. Verified directly against the rendered DOM ([1.3.1](#1.3.1)).
5. **The brand's hot-pink "highlight" color (`#ff1493`) fails text contrast** at the sizes it's actually used at for handwritten annotations and captions — several of these annotations carry real content, not just decorative flourish ([1.4.3](#1.4.3)).
6. **The draggable "digi-stickers" on the About page are mouse/touch-only** — no keyboard path, no non-dragging alternative, and several drag targets are smaller than the WCAG 2.2 minimum target size ([2.1.1](#2.1.1), [2.5.7](#2.5.7), [2.5.8](#2.5.8)).

None of these require re-architecting the site. Most are component-level or single-file fixes (see [Section 7](#7-prioritized-remediation-plan)).

## 3. Methodology

This audit combined two methods:

1. **Code-level review** of every route and component in the repository (`app/`, `components/`, `lib/projects.ts`, `app/globals.css`), including a full read of the case-study content model so every image's `alt` text, every annotation, and every generated heading could be checked against its actual rendered output rather than guessed at.
2. **Live verification in a rendered browser** against a local instance of the site, used specifically to confirm findings that cannot be reliably determined from source alone:
   - Rendered heading outline (`document.querySelectorAll('h1..h6')`) on a case study page, to confirm the heading-hierarchy finding rather than inferring it from the content heuristic in [ContentSection.tsx](components/ContentSection.tsx).
   - Focus geometry: focusing an in-page link via `scrollIntoView` + `.focus()` and measuring its bounding box against the sticky header's bounding box, to confirm focus obscuring.
   - Reflow at a 320×700 CSS-pixel viewport (equivalent to 400% zoom on a 1280px design, per the WCAG 1.4.10 test method) on every page template, checking `document.documentElement.scrollWidth` against `clientWidth`.
   - Mobile menu behavior: opening the menu, dispatching `Escape`, and confirming focus return to the trigger button.
   - Rendered `<title>` on every route.
   - Computed CSS custom properties, to confirm the design tokens used in contrast calculations match what actually ships.

Color contrast was calculated against the design tokens defined in [app/globals.css](app/globals.css) (`--canvas: #f7f4ef`, `--ink: #16233a`, `--accent: #3e6fa6`, `--highlight: #ff1493`, `--neutral: #6b6459`) using the WCAG relative luminance formula, cross-checked against the live computed styles.

No assistive technology (screen reader, switch device) was used directly; findings involving screen reader behavior are based on WCAG success criteria and standard AT-mapping conventions (ARIA semantics, accessible name computation) rather than a live AT session. This should be treated as a strong first-pass audit, not a substitute for testing with actual AT users before a public/professional release.

## 5. Detailed Findings

### <a name="2.4.2"></a>Finding 1 — WCAG 2.4.2 Page Titled
**Severity:** High
**Location:** Site-wide — [app/layout.tsx:29-33](app/layout.tsx#L29-L33); no page overrides it.

**Description:** Every route renders the exact same `<title>Emma H. Tandle — UX & Product Designer</title>`. Verified by fetching the raw HTML of `/`, `/work`, `/about`, `/contact`, `/work/skytsengel`, and `/work/caritas-faellesskab` — all six return an identical `<title>` tag. There is only one `metadata` export in the whole codebase (the root layout); no route defines `generateMetadata` or its own `metadata`.

**Why it fails:** 2.4.2 requires page titles that describe topic or purpose, so users can tell pages apart in browser tabs, history, bookmarks, and (for screen reader users) when switching between open tabs or announcing page load. With one title for eight distinct pages, none of those orientation cues work — a user with several case studies open in tabs, or a screen reader user who just navigated from `/work` to a case study, gets no title-based confirmation of where they landed.

**Recommended fix:** Add per-route `generateMetadata`. For static routes (`about`, `contact`, `work`), a `metadata` export naming the page (e.g. `"About — Emma H. Tandle"`). For `app/work/[project]/page.tsx`, a `generateMetadata` function using `project.title` (e.g. `"${project.title} — Emma H. Tandle"`), reusing the same `projects.find()` lookup already in the page component.

### <a name="2.4.1"></a>Finding 2 — WCAG 2.4.1 Bypass Blocks
**Severity:** High
**Location:** Site-wide — [app/layout.tsx](app/layout.tsx), [components/SiteHeader.tsx](components/SiteHeader.tsx)

**Description:** There is no mechanism to skip the repeated header navigation and jump directly to a page's main content. Every route repeats the same `<SiteHeader>` (a `<nav>` with 2–3 links) before `{children}`, with no skip link, no `<main id>` anchor exposed as a bypass target, and no landmark-based shortcut offered.

**Why it fails:** 2.4.1 requires a way to bypass blocks of content that repeat across pages. Keyboard users currently must Tab through the same 2–3 nav links (or open/close the mobile menu, on small viewports) on every single page before reaching unique content — trivial on this site today, but a textbook 2.4.1 failure and worse as the site grows.

**Recommended fix:** Add a visually-hidden-until-focused "Skip to main content" link as the very first focusable element in `app/layout.tsx`, pointing to an `id="main-content"` added to each page's `<main>`. Standard pattern: `sr-only focus:not-sr-only focus:absolute` positioning, matching the existing focus-ring styling already used elsewhere in the codebase for consistency.

### <a name="2.4.11"></a>Finding 3 — WCAG 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum)
**Severity:** High
**Location:** Site-wide — [components/SiteHeader.tsx:77](components/SiteHeader.tsx#L77) (`sticky top-0 z-50`)

**Description:** The site header is `position: sticky; top: 0; z-index: 50` with an opaque `bg-canvas` background and no compensating `scroll-margin-top`/`scroll-padding-top` anywhere in [app/globals.css](app/globals.css) (confirmed absent via full-codebase search). Verified live: focusing a link partway down the Skytsengel case study page and letting the browser scroll it into view placed the link's top edge at `y = 24px` while the sticky header's bottom edge sat at `y = 92px` — the focused element was rendered entirely underneath the header.

**Why it fails:** 2.4.11 (new in WCAG 2.2) requires that when an element receives keyboard focus, it isn't entirely hidden by author-created sticky content. A keyboard user tabbing through the page loses sight of exactly which link or button is currently focused whenever that focus lands near the top of the viewport — precisely the moment they most need visual confirmation.

**Recommended fix:** Add `scroll-margin-top` (roughly the header's rendered height, e.g. `132px` at desktop / measure per breakpoint) to focusable elements, or more simply to `:focus-visible` globally, or to a shared scroll-padding-top on `html`. Since the header height is responsive (mobile vs. desktop nav differ), consider driving the value from a CSS custom property set to the header's actual height so it stays in sync automatically.

### <a name="1.3.1"></a>Finding 4 — WCAG 1.3.1 Info and Relationships (heading structure)
**Severity:** High
**Location:** Case studies with `processTimeline`, `interviewPanel`, or `guidelines` data — currently Skytsengel and Caritas Fællesskab. Components: [ProcessTimeline.tsx:41](components/ProcessTimeline.tsx#L41), [InterviewPanel.tsx:36](components/InterviewPanel.tsx#L36), [GuidelineList.tsx:15](components/GuidelineList.tsx#L15), interacting with the subheading heuristic in [ContentSection.tsx:26-31](components/ContentSection.tsx#L26-L31).

**Description:** `ProcessTimeline`, `InterviewPanel`, and `GuidelineList` each unconditionally render their item titles as `<h4>`, while `ContentSection` separately promotes short, unpunctuated paragraph chunks (its own subheading heuristic — see note below) to `<h3>`. On the rendered Caritas Fællesskab page, the actual DOM heading order is:

```
H1: Caritas Fællesskab
H2: Challenge
H2: Process
H4: Fieldwork              ← ProcessTimeline stage — skips from H2 to H4
H4: Interviews
H4: Co-Design Workshop
H4: Guidelines
H3: Fieldwork               ← ContentSection subheading — same label, different level
H3: Interviews
H4: Caritas Workers          ← InterviewPanel group — again skips a level
H4: Volunteers
H4: Participants
H3: Co-Design Workshop
H2: Outcome
H4: One official platform    ← GuidelineList item — H2 straight to H4, six times
H4: Support, don't replace
H4: Minimize barriers
H4: Personalization
H4: Right time, right information
H4: Designated spaces
```
(Confirmed by querying the live-rendered DOM, not inferred from source.)

**Why it fails:** 1.3.1 requires that structural relationships conveyed visually (here, section → subsection nesting) also be conveyed programmatically, and heading levels are the primary mechanism screen reader users rely on to navigate and understand document structure without reading linearly. This outline skips levels repeatedly (H2→H4) and, worse, uses the *same visible label* ("Fieldwork", "Interviews", "Co-Design Workshop") at two different heading levels in immediate succession — a screen reader user navigating by heading gets a structurally nonsensical, duplicated outline rather than a a clear content map of the case study.

**Separately worth noting:** the `<h3>` subheadings themselves aren't authored directly — `ContentSection.isSubheading()` infers "this paragraph chunk is actually a subheading" from formatting alone (≤30 characters, no terminal punctuation, no newline). This is a content-driven guess rather than an explicit content-model field, which works today but is fragile: a short, punctuation-free sentence in future case-study copy would silently become a heading.

**Recommended fix:**
- Nest correctly: since `ProcessTimeline`/`InterviewPanel`/`GuidelineList` render *inside* a section already headed by the `ContentSection`'s `<h2>` (and, for `ProcessTimeline`/`InterviewPanel`, alongside `<h3>` subheadings of the same section), their item titles should be `<h3>` when no other `<h3>` precedes them in that section, and the whole scheme should avoid rendering the same label at two levels. The cleanest fix is to make heading level a prop passed down from the page (matching the pattern `ProjectCard` and `ContentSection` already use for `headingLevel`), rather than hard-coded per component.
- Consider promoting `isSubheading` from a text heuristic to an explicit field on the content model (`lib/projects.ts`) — e.g. marking subheadings with `\n\n## Interviews\n\n` or a small structured array — so heading structure is authored intentionally rather than inferred.

### <a name="1.4.3"></a>Finding 5 — WCAG 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum)
**Severity:** High
**Location:** [CaseStudyHero.tsx:40](components/CaseStudyHero.tsx#L40) (`titleNote`, all case studies), [MediaFrame.tsx:28-32](components/MediaFrame.tsx#L28-L32) (`base`/`lg` annotation sizes, used throughout case-study images), [ProcessTimeline.tsx:29](components/ProcessTimeline.tsx#L29) and [InterviewPanel.tsx:22](components/InterviewPanel.tsx#L22) (side notes), [app/page.tsx:22-25](app/page.tsx#L22-L25) (homepage tagline).

**Description:** The `--highlight` token (`#ff1493`, hot pink) on `--canvas` (`#f7f4ef`, warm ivory) — the color used for every `Scribble` and annotation — measures **3.32:1**. That clears the 3:1 threshold for *large* text (≥24px, or ≥18.66px bold) and for non-text UI, but not the 4.5:1 threshold required for normal text. The `--highlight` token is explicitly commented in `globals.css` as "large text / non-text emphasis only" — a constraint the design system states but several real components don't honor:

| Usage | Rendered size | Weight | Qualifies as "large text"? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homepage tagline ("I design digital experiences…") | `text-xl` = 20px | Caveat 600 | Ambiguous — below the 24px unweighted threshold; 600 is semi-bold, not conventionally "bold" (700), so this is at best borderline |
| `CaseStudyHero` `titleNote` (e.g. "translates to 'guardian angel'") | `text-lg` = 18px | Caveat 600 | **No** — below both the 24px and 18.66px-bold thresholds regardless of weight interpretation |
| `MediaFrame` annotation, `size: "base"` (default) | `text-lg` = 18px | Caveat 600 | **No**, same reason |
| `ProcessTimeline` / `InterviewPanel` side notes | `text-base` = 16px | Caveat 600 | **No** |
| Larger scribbles (`text-2xl` = 24px, e.g. "scroll down to see my work") | 24px | Caveat 600 | Yes — passes at 3:1 |

**Why it fails:** These aren't purely decorative flourishes — the `titleNote` communicates real information ("translates to 'guardian angel'"), and image annotations at the default `base` size point out specific interface features (e.g. "custom redesigned logo", "home page" on the Skytsengel hero mockup). Text conveying real content at 18px/16px, rendered at 3.32:1, is below the threshold low-vision users need to read it reliably.

**Recommended fix:** Either (a) darken `--highlight` for text usage to hit 4.5:1 against `--canvas` (a hue around the same pink family typically needs to drop to roughly `#c2106e`–`#b8106b` territory to clear 4.5:1 — verify against the actual token before shipping), or (b) enforce the "large text only" rule the code comment already states by bumping `titleNote`, annotation `base` size, and the `ProcessTimeline`/`InterviewPanel` notes up to at least `text-2xl` (24px), or (c) introduce a second, darker token (e.g. `--highlight-text`) reserved specifically for any highlight-colored text under 24px, keeping `#ff1493` for large/decorative use. Also worth resolving the ambiguity on `text-xl` (20px) at Caveat 600 explicitly rather than leaving it borderline.

### <a name="2.2.2"></a>Finding 6 — WCAG 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide
**Severity:** Medium
**Location:** About page — [components/CollageVideo.tsx](components/CollageVideo.tsx), used twice (`/about/iced-latte.mp4`, `/about/playing-music.mp4`)

**Description:** `CollageVideo` autoplays on mount, loops indefinitely, and provides no visible control to pause it. It does check `prefers-reduced-motion` before calling `.play()` — a genuinely good practice — but that only helps users who have set that OS-level preference.

**Why it fails:** 2.2.2 requires a mechanism to pause, stop, or hide any moving content that starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds, and plays alongside other content — regardless of whether the user has a system-level motion preference configured. A user who finds looping motion distracting but hasn't (or doesn't know how to) set `prefers-reduced-motion` at the OS level has no in-page way to stop these clips.

**Recommended fix:** Add a small pause/play toggle button overlaid on each `CollageVideo` (or on the enclosing `Sticker`), with a clear accessible name ("Pause video" / "Play video"). This also resolves the `aria-hidden="true"` currently on the `<video>` — once the video is user-controllable, it's no longer purely decorative and the element (or a wrapping control) needs to be exposed to assistive technology, not hidden.

### <a name="1.4.10-about"></a>Finding 7 — WCAG 1.4.10 Reflow (About page)
**Severity:** Medium
**Location:** About page — the handwritten `Scribble` captions inside the collage, e.g. [app/about/page.tsx:190-192](app/about/page.tsx#L190-L192), [:215-217](app/about/page.tsx#L215-L217), [:259-261](app/about/page.tsx#L259-L261) (`whitespace-nowrap` + absolute positioning)

**Description:** At a 320×700 CSS-pixel viewport (the standard WCAG reflow test width, equivalent to 400% zoom on a 1280px design), the About page's `scrollWidth` measures 320px vs. a 320px `clientWidth` in most areas, but overflows to **388px** overall. Live inspection traced the overflow to several `whitespace-nowrap`, absolutely-positioned `Scribble` captions (e.g. "getting crafty", "snowboarding", "singing") that sit near the edge of their parent `Sticker` in the wrapped collage layout — `whitespace-nowrap` prevents them from wrapping onto a second line as the viewport narrows, so they push past the right edge instead.

**Why it fails:** 1.4.10 requires content to reflow to fit the viewport width without requiring two-dimensional scrolling. This produces a genuine horizontal scrollbar on the About page at narrow widths / high zoom — exactly the case 1.4.10 exists to prevent for low-vision users who zoom in rather than shrinking their browser window.

**Recommended fix:** Drop `whitespace-nowrap` on these caption `Scribble`s at narrow viewports (e.g. only apply it from `sm:` up), or constrain them with a `max-w-[…]` matching the sticker's own width so they wrap instead of overflowing.

### <a name="sticker"></a>Finding 8 — Draggable stickers are pointer-only and undersized (WCAG 2.1.1, 2.5.1, 2.5.7, 2.5.8, 4.1.2)
**Severity:** Medium
**Location:** About page — [components/Sticker.tsx](components/Sticker.tsx), wrapping the 10 decorative doodle "digi-stickers" and the two `CollageVideo` clips

**Description:** `Sticker` implements drag-to-reposition purely through `onPointerDown`/`onPointerMove`/`onPointerUp`. Verified live: the rendered `<div>` has `tabIndex="-1"`, no `role`, and no `aria-label` — it is not part of the keyboard focus order and exposes no accessible name or role. Several stickers wrap small icons (`h-4 w-4` = 16px, `h-5 w-5` = 20px), both under the 24×24px minimum.

**Why it fails, mapped to specific criteria:**
- **2.1.1 Keyboard** — the drag interaction has no keyboard equivalent at all; it's unreachable and unusable without a pointer.
- **2.5.1 Pointer Gestures** — dragging is a path-based gesture with no single-pointer, non-dragging alternative offered.
- **2.5.7 Dragging Movements** (new in 2.2) — requires that any drag-based function also be operable via a single pointer without dragging (e.g. tap-to-pick-up, tap-to-place); none exists here.
- **2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum)** (new in 2.2) — the smaller sticker instances (16px, 20px) fall under the 24×24px CSS pixel minimum for pointer targets.
- **4.1.2 Name, Role, Value** — as an interactive control, the sticker exposes no role or accessible name to assistive technology.

**Context:** the copy explicitly invites interaction ("play around with my digi-stickers"), so this is a real, if playful, feature — not incidental chrome. Because it's decorative rather than gating access to content (the underlying images and captions remain readable without dragging anything), this is rated Medium rather than High.

**Recommended fix:** This is the hardest fix in the report to do well, since the feature is inherently pointer-flavored. Reasonable options: (a) make stickers focusable and keyboard-operable (arrow keys to nudge position once focused, matching a widely-used pattern for draggable UI), and/or (b) treat the drag as an enhancement layered on top of a fully static, already-fine default layout — i.e. don't require interaction to access anything, and accept that the *drag itself* just isn't available without a pointer, documenting it as a deliberate, scoped exception. If pursuing (b), ensure the sticker is at minimum not a keyboard trap or focus hazard (currently it isn't, since it's simply skipped — `tabIndex="-1"` — which is an acceptable stopgap but doesn't resolve 2.5.7/2.5.8 for pointer users). Also bump the smallest icon instances to at least 24×24px regardless of which direction is taken.

### <a name="1.4.11"></a>Finding 9 — WCAG 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast
**Severity:** Low–Medium
**Location:** Work page — [components/ProjectFilters.tsx:44-48](components/ProjectFilters.tsx#L44-L48), unselected filter button state (`border border-neutral/30`)

**Description:** The unselected filter buttons ("UX Design", "Product Design", etc.) rely on a `neutral` border at 30% opacity against the `canvas` background to convey their boundary. Calculated: `#6b6459` at 30% alpha over `#f7f4ef` resolves to roughly **1.5:1** contrast — well under the 3:1 minimum for UI component boundaries.

**Why it fails:** 1.4.11 requires visual boundaries needed to identify interactive UI components to reach 3:1 contrast against adjacent colors. Filter buttons remain operable (they have a text label, adequate 44px target size, and clear `aria-pressed`/focus states), so this doesn't block use — but the faint border alone doesn't reliably signal "this is a button" to low-vision users who can perceive the label but not the boundary.

**Recommended fix:** Increase the unselected-state border to at least `neutral/60` or switch to a solid lower-opacity `ink`/`neutral` tone that clears 3:1 against `canvas`.

### <a name="1.4.10-home"></a>Finding 10 — WCAG 1.4.10 Reflow (Homepage, minor)
**Severity:** Low
**Location:** Homepage — [app/page.tsx:67](app/page.tsx#L67) (decorative `Sparkle` beside the Contact button)

**Description:** At 320px viewport width, one decorative `Sparkle` SVG next to the "Contact" button (`className="absolute -right-6 -top-3 h-5 w-5 rotate-12 text-highlight"`) is not hidden at small breakpoints (unlike most other decorative sparkles on the page, which use `hidden sm:block`/`lg:block`), and its `-right-6` offset pushes it slightly past the viewport edge, producing a 10px horizontal scroll on the homepage at 320px.

**Why it fails:** Same 1.4.10 requirement as Finding 7, but purely decorative (`aria-hidden="true"`) and far smaller in impact — no content is lost, just a faint horizontal scrollbar.

**Recommended fix:** Add `hidden sm:block` (or similar) to this sparkle to match the pattern already used elsewhere on the same page, or pull its offset in.

### <a name="1.1.1"></a>Finding 11 — WCAG 1.1.1 Non-text Content (annotation integration)
**Severity:** Low
**Location:** Case-study hero and inline images with `annotations`, e.g. Skytsengel's hero mockup ([lib/projects.ts:102-115](lib/projects.ts#L102-L115))

**Description:** Alt text throughout `lib/projects.ts` is consistently thorough and well-written — this is a genuine strength of the site (see [Section 6](#6-passed-criteria)). The one gap: several hero/case-study images carry visual callout annotations pointing at specific regions of the image (e.g., an arrow + "custom redesigned logo" pointing at one part of the Skytsengel mockup, "home page" pointing at another), but the image's own `alt` text stays generic ("Mockup of the redesigned Skytsengel app interface") and doesn't mention what the callouts point to.

**Why it fails:** The annotation text itself *is* present as real, readable text in the DOM (not hidden), so nothing is fully inaccessible — but the spatial correspondence between callout and image region (which a sighted user gets for free from the arrow) isn't conveyed to a screen reader user, who encounters the callout as a disconnected phrase near, but not integrated with, the image's description.

**Recommended fix:** Low priority, but worth folding the callout content into the image's `alt` text where it's meaningful (e.g. `"Mockup of the redesigned Skytsengel app interface, highlighting the custom redesigned logo and the home page"`), rather than relying solely on the visually-positioned annotation.

### <a name="2.4.3-back-to-top"></a>Finding 12 — WCAG 2.4.3 Focus Order (Back to top link)
**Severity:** Low
**Location:** Footer — [components/Footer.tsx:88-93](components/Footer.tsx#L88-L93)

**Description:** The "Back to top" link uses `href="#"`. This scrolls the viewport to the top in most browsers, but keyboard focus stays on the footer link itself rather than moving to the top of the page — so a keyboard user's next Tab press continues from the bottom of the document, not the top, even though the page has visually scrolled.

**Why it fails:** Not a strict SC violation on its own, but a real friction point under the spirit of 2.4.3 (Focus Order): the visual state (scrolled to top) and the keyboard state (focus still at the bottom) diverge, which is disorienting for sighted keyboard users in particular.

**Recommended fix:** Point the link at a real anchor (`href="#top"` with a corresponding `id="top"` near the header, or the skip-link target from Finding 2) and move focus there programmatically, or convert it to a button that calls `scrollTo` + `.focus()` on the header/logo link.
